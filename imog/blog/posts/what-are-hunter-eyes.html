What Are Hunter Eyes?

Nov 26, 2026 · 7 min read
Hunter Eyes

Hunter eyes are one of the most viral looksmaxxing eye-area ideals. Here’s what the term actually means, which traits create the look, and how to evaluate your own eye area without forum mythology.

Hunter eyes meaning

“Hunter eyes” usually describes a deeper-set, narrower-looking eye area with:

  • Lower upper-eyelid exposure
  • Stronger brow support / brow bone presence
  • Neutral-to-positive canthal tilt
  • A sharper, more hooded or compact eye shape in photos

It’s contrasted online with “prey eyes” — larger, rounder, higher-lid-exposure eyes. Those labels are slang, not medical terms.

Traits that create the hunter-eye look

  • Upper lid exposure: less visible lid often reads more “deep-set”
  • Canthal tilt: slight positive tilt is commonly preferred in the aesthetic
  • Brow position: lower, denser brows reinforce the look
  • Facial fat: leaner faces show orbital structure more clearly

How to check your eye area

Use a straight-on scan, not a flexed selfie. Look at lid show, tilt, and whether soft tissue is hiding structure. iMog’s secondary traits (including canthal tilt / eye shape style labels) help you name what you’re seeing.

Hunter eyes are a cluster of traits — not a single checkbox.

What you can improve

  • Body-fat reduction for a sharper orbital read
  • Sleep and allergy management (less puff)
  • Brow grooming that fits your bone structure
  • Photo lighting that doesn’t erase deep-set shadows incorrectly

Bone-deep eye socket shape is mostly genetic. Softmaxxing still changes how the eye area photographs.

FAQ

Are hunter eyes required for a high PSL score?

No. They’re one aesthetic lane. Many high-scoring faces don’t match the meme template.

Do positive canthal tilt and hunter eyes mean the same thing?

No. Tilt is one ingredient. Read Positive vs Negative Canthal Tilt.
