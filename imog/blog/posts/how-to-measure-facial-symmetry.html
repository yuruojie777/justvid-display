How to Measure Facial Symmetry

Nov 26, 2026 · 7 min read
Facial Symmetry

Symmetry is one of the most searched face-measurement topics — and one of the easiest to overthink. Here’s a practical guide on how to measure facial symmetry without turning into a caliper account.

What facial symmetry means

Facial symmetry is how closely the left and right sides of your face mirror each other. Eyes, brows, smile line, and jaw corners are the usual checkpoints. Perfect mirror symmetry is rare — and not required for an attractive face.

Quick DIY measurement (photo method)

  1. Take a straight-on photo at eye level
  2. Draw a vertical midline down the center of the nose
  3. Compare eye height, brow height, and mouth corners left vs right
  4. Note jaw corner height and width differences

You’re looking for obvious imbalances, not pixel-perfect equality.

AI measurement

AI face rating tools estimate symmetry as part of the overall Attractiveness Score. In iMog, facial symmetry appears alongside other traits, so you can see whether it’s a real weak point or a non-issue.

If your symmetry score is “fine” but skin or jaw is weak, fix the bigger lever first.

What causes asymmetry

  • Normal developmental variation (most people)
  • Sleeping position / soft-tissue puffiness
  • Dental / bite factors
  • Injuries or habitual expressions
  • Camera angle pretending to be asymmetry

Can you improve facial symmetry?

You can often improve how symmetrical you look:

  • Reduce facial puffiness (sleep, salt, alcohol)
  • Fix posture and head tilt in photos
  • Balanced chewing habits over time
  • Hair and brow shaping to visually balance sides

Bone-level asymmetry has limits. Measure, prioritize, then move on.

FAQ

Does higher symmetry always mean higher PSL?

No. Harmony and feature quality can outweigh mild asymmetry.
